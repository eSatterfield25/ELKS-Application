Phone's and Tablets Have many ways to connect Wired and wireless connectivity Standards and options change through the years Used for synchronization, connectivity, backup, and identification sometimes simultaneously Common ways to connect Older devices (mini& micro) USB A and USB-C (more common in modern devices) USB-C uses double sided 24 pin acts as USB 2.0/3.0/3.1/4 connection (different signals using the same connector can also transmit DisplayPort, HDMI, and Thunderbolt signals Designed to be easy to use and is using faster speeds Apple uses lightning is slightly better than older USB's NFC- Near Field Communication sends small amounts of data wirelessly Built into your phone Payment system, transportation, in-person information exchange Access token / identity card (your phone can be used to replace a ID card) Bluetooth- Highspeed communication over short distances PAN (personal area network Connects mobile devices Smartphones, tethering, headsets and headphones, health monitors, automobile and phone integrations, smartwatches, external speakers Hotspot / tethering (Using your phone as a router) Hotspot - having multiple devices connecting to your phone at once tethering - having one device connected to your phone.
