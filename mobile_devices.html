Mobile Devices: Phones and Tablets

Phones and tablets have many ways to connect with eachother and other devices

    1. Wired and wireless connectivity
    2. Standards and options change through the years
  • Used for synchronization, connectivity, backup, and identification (sometimes simultaneously)

Common ways to connect

Cables

Over the years, there have been many different types of cables. While some have become more obsolete than others, it is still important to know about them has you may run accross them and need them when working in IT

  • Older devices (mini& micro)
  • USB A and USB-C (USB-C being more common in modern devices)
  • USB-C uses double sided 24 pin
    • acts as USB 2.0/3.0/3.1/4 connection (different signals using the same connector)
    • can also transmit DisplayPort, HDMI, and Thunderbolt signals
    • Designed to be easy to use and is using faster speeds
  • Apple uses lightning
    • is slightly better than older USB's

NFC-

  • Near Field Communication
  • sends small amounts of data wirelessly
  • Built into your phone
    • Payment system
    • transportation
    • in-person information exchange
  • Access token / identity card
    • (your phone can be used to replace a ID card)

Bluetooth-

  • Highspeed communication over short distances
  • PAN (personal area network
  • Connects mobile devices
    • Smartphones
    • headsets and headphones
    • health monitors
    • automobile and phone integrations
    • smartwatches
    • external speakers

Hotspot / tethering (Using your phone as a router)

  • Hotspot - having multiple devices connecting to your phone at once
  • tethering - having one device connected to your phone.

Stylus

Writing / drawing utensil used for phones / tablets. (used to get more accurate writing)
